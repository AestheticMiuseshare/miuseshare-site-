My Artistic Perspective

音乐 · 歌词 · 画面

Perspective in Brief

My artistic perspective falls into three parts: music, lyrics and imagery. Music is about coordination and regional meter; lyrics carry thought, feeling and the spirit of a place; imagery is about form, meaning and color. Together, the three parts show how this aesthetic system takes shape in my own work.

I. Music

1. Coordination among voices

When several voices play together, the meter stays coordinated and the whole stays harmonious. When choosing instruments, I consider whether their tone fits the other voices; the tones set off and reinforce one another. Instrumentation serves the balance of the voices.

2. Meter chosen by regional culture and style

Meter is chosen according to the musical character of a region and the style in question. Each region and each style has its own distinctive meter; once the meter is set, the instrumentation and vocal techniques follow that regional style.

3. Intro, interlude and outro are extensions of the main melody

The intro first reveals the key and mood of the melody; the interlude leaves room for the piece and the listener to let the mood settle, before the repetition returns with fuller reception, while deepening the listener's feeling; the outro is the lingering resonance after the closing. All three are an extension and development of the main melody.

II. Lyrics

1. Lyrics carry thought

While expressing feeling, lyrics seek resonance with the listener.

2. Psychology and feeling

Some singles look at people's psychological needs and emotional states, at the situations inside the heart.

3. Travel writing as material, revealing the spirit of a place

Material is drawn from the humanities, history and social culture of different places; through the humanistic and historical life of one place, the spirit of that place comes through.

III. Imagery

1. Form: composition, layering, depth, grain

On the formal side, I first look at whether the composition highlights the subject and holds a sense of balance, whether front-to-back layering and depth are opened up, and whether the texture is sufficient. Composition attends to beauty while arranging the emphasis of information across different parts of the image. These judgments come from more than ten years of photographic practice.

2. Content: conveying a message, with meaning and implication

Beyond looking pleasing, an image should let people see what it is saying, and whether there is something worth reflecting on behind it.

3. Color: supporting mood, mindful of the era's culture

Color serves as a support to the image, working for the expression of mood; the choice of color also takes account of the cultural background of the era.
